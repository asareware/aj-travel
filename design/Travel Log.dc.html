Aj's London, Sorted Day by Day

Four nights, five days — bridges and boats, a Chelsea away-day watch party, and the West African corners of South London. Built around the Park Plaza Waterloo.

Pack a light rain layer — always a fair bet in London
What to wearLayers you can peel off, one packable rain shell, and shoes that survive an eight-hour day.
The one rule

London changes its mind faster than you can get back to the hotel. A packable waterproof lives in your bag every single day, whatever it looked like out of the window at breakfast. Not the good jacket — the one you would not mind wringing out.

The daily base

Dark jeans or chinos, a tee with a shirt or fine knit over the top. September afternoons sit around 70°F and drop to the mid-50s once the sun goes, so the layer you shed at noon is the one you want at nine.

Shoes

You will walk much further than the plan makes it look — Rye Lane, the South Bank, Borough Market, the whole of Day 3. One pair has to be comfortable enough for that and dark and clean enough for a Soho door, which rules out running shoes.

Going out

Soho leans smart-casual and the doors do actually turn people away: a collared shirt or a dress, nothing sportswear-shaped. Shoreditch on Day 2 is looser and will not care.

Match day

You are watching Arsenal v Chelsea in a room that is entirely Chelsea. Blue if you want to belong in it. Nothing red, which in that pub reads as the wrong side of the fixture.

The work days

Thursday and Friday you are on camera from 2:30pm. Anything that reads clean on video from the waist up does the job — no one is asking you to change twice.

Worth knowing4 notes on this plan — timing, bookings, and the things that caught us out
TZ
The time-zone math is 5 hours, not 4: both the US and UK are in their own daylight-saving time in September, so London runs a full 5 hours ahead of Eastern. Your 9:30am–5:30pm Eastern workday lands as 2:30pm–10:30pm London time — it eats the entire afternoon and evening of Thursday and Friday. Days 1 and 2 are built around that: sightseeing before you log on, and Tower Bridge moved to Sunday morning, where the weekend is work-free. Nothing got cut, just reshuffled into the hours you actually have.
Sale
The ticket window has closed: Chelsea's members' sale for the Arsenal away tickets ran on Wednesday 26 August — loyalty-point windows at 10am, 12pm and 2pm, then any leftovers to all members from 4pm. Away allocations are tiny, and this itinerary never depended on landing one: Day 4 is built around the pub, not the turnstile. If you did get a ticket, the Emirates is on the Piccadilly line — Arsenal station sits practically at the ground — and The Butcher's Hook still works as the post-match stop.
+
It's Arsenal away, not Stamford Bridge: kickoff is at the Emirates Stadium in North London, so there's no "go to the ground" option even with a ticket in hand — away allocations are tiny. The move that actually gets you around a wall of Chelsea fans is watching from a pub right by Stamford Bridge, where the crowd gathers for away games too. Full plan on Day 4.
!
Brixton after dark, with one exception: the outdoor stretch — Electric Avenue, Windrush Square, wandering the streets — is scheduled in daylight, same street sense as anywhere with a lively nightlife scene after dusk. The one thing that runs into evening is dinner, and that's inside Brixton Village Market — a busy, covered, well-lit arcade, not open street. Head there and out again by taxi or tube rather than lingering outside afterward.

Marrakech, Day by Day

Six days built on a friend's original plan — beach club, Agafay desert camp, a hot air balloon, and her birthday on day five — with the open stretches (rest blocks, free afternoons, the nightcap) worked out and locked in ahead of time.

What to wearLoose and covered in the city; wear what you like inside the riad, the beach club and the camp.
The rule that matters

Marrakech is a conservative Muslim city and the medina is where that shows most. Shoulders and knees covered, loose rather than tight — long linen trousers, a maxi skirt, a midi dress. Nobody enforces this and nobody will stop you, but it is the difference between moving through the souks easily and being looked at all afternoon. You will also be cooler in it than in shorts.

Where it relaxes

Private space plays by its own rules. Inside the riad, at the beach club, at the desert camp and in most restaurants, wear whatever you want — swimwear at the pool included. The cover-up goes back on for the drive there and back.

The temperature swing

Early November runs about 78°F in the afternoon and mid-50s at night, and Agafay drops further than the city does. Every day is a layers day, including the ones that start hot enough to make that feel unnecessary.

Shoes

The medina underfoot is uneven, dusty, and wet in places you will not see coming. Closed shoes you can walk a long way in, and nothing you would mind scuffing. Sandals are for the pool.

Sun

The light is stronger than the temperature suggests, and two of these days are spent fully exposed. Sunglasses always; something for your head on the desert and balloon mornings.

The dressy night

The birthday dinner is the one night to actually dress. Marrakech's golden hour is low, warm and forgiving, and solid colours hold up against the pink walls and tilework far better than busy prints do.
